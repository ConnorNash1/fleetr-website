Winter operations for rental fleets in Canada

Canadian winters test rental fleets: tires, storms, cancelled flights and salt. Here's how independent rental companies keep cars safe and rentals running from November to April.

By the fleetr team. November 17, 2026.

Winter tires and the rules

Winter tires matter more than any other winter decision, for renters' safety and your liability. The rules differ by province: Quebec requires winter tires on passenger vehicles in winter, British Columbia requires them (or chains) on many highways in the colder months, and most other provinces, including the Atlantic provinces, don't require them but strongly recommend them. Rules on studded tires also vary by province and season. Check the current rules wherever your cars are registered and driven.

Whatever the law, renters expect winter tires. Many independents put winter tires on the whole fleet from November to April; others keep a set of classes on them. Record which cars have them, so staff and renters know before pickup, and mention it when you confirm a winter booking.

Preparing the cars

  • A snow brush and ice scraper in every car, checked at each turnaround.
  • Winter washer fluid, topped up at every turnaround; renters run through it quickly.
  • Batteries tested before the cold sets in. A dead battery on a 7:00 pickup costs you the rental and the renter's goodwill.
  • Wipers replaced in the fall.
  • Cars cleared of snow before pickup. A car buried in a drift is not "ready".

At pickup, take a minute to tell winter renters what visitors often don't know: that the car has winter tires (or doesn't), where the brush is, to clear the whole roof and lights before driving, and to leave extra time and distance on icy roads. Renters from places without real winters, and people renting while their own car is in the shop after a winter crash, are the ones who most need it. A short note in the booking text helps too.

Storm days

A big storm moves everything at once: pickups delayed, flights cancelled, returns stuck at the airport, staff who can't get in. Prepare for it:

  • Text early. Tell renters with pickups and returns on the storm day what to do before they ask.
  • Make changes easy. Let renters move their pickup or return from a link, rather than all calling at once.
  • Waive what's fair. Many operators don't charge extra days for returns delayed by a road closure or a cancelled flight. Decide your policy before the storm, not during it.
  • Watch the next days. Storms push demand into the following days; check your availability for them.

Late returns and extensions

In winter, more renters are late through no fault of their own. Keep your overdue process firm but fair: an automatic text after the return time, a grace period, then a call. A renter who replies "stuck in the storm, back tomorrow" is not the same as one who's gone quiet, and your system should show the difference.

Salt and spring

Road salt corrodes brakes, lines and bodywork. Wash cars regularly through the winter, including underneath, and check brakes at every service. In spring, swap tires back, inspect for corrosion and chips, and fix them before the summer rush, when every car needs to be on the road.

A season checklist

October: book tire changes early, before every shop is full; test batteries; replace wipers; stock brushes, scrapers and winter washer fluid; write down your storm-day policy and share it with staff.

November: winter tires on, and marked on each car's record; check block heater cords where you have them; tell renters in your booking texts which cars have winter tires.

Through the winter: clear snow before every pickup, wash regularly to fight salt, and check the forecast every evening for the next day's pickups and returns.

April: summer tires back on once the risk of snow has passed, a corrosion and chip inspection, and repairs done before the summer rush.

Questions people ask

Do rental cars in Canada have winter tires?

Many do from November to April, and Quebec requires them in winter. Ask the company, or check the listing, which cars have them.

Are winter tires mandatory in Atlantic Canada?

Not in most Atlantic provinces, but they're strongly recommended. Rules change, so check the current provincial rules.

What happens if a storm delays my rental return?

Contact the company as soon as you can. Many operators don't charge extra for returns delayed by road closures or cancelled flights.

Know which cars have winter tires.

Mark each car's winter tires and keep storm-day changes to every rental in one place.
