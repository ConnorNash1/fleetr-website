How to start a car rental business in Canada

A step-by-step guide for anyone thinking about renting out cars in Canada, from choosing your customers to taking your first booking, written by people who build rental software with Canadian operators.

By the fleetr team. November 17, 2026.

Car rental looks simple from the counter: cars, keys, a form. Behind it is insurance that most brokers don't write every day, cars that lose value every month, and a cash cycle that starts with buying the vehicles. Here is how to start one in Canada without the expensive surprises.

1. Choose who you rent to

The big brands own the airport counters. Independent companies win somewhere else, and choosing where shapes every decision after this one.

  • Insurance replacement. Drivers whose car is in the body shop after a claim, booked by adjusters and shops. Steady, weekday-heavy, paid on account.
  • Local and neighbourhood rentals. People whose own car is in for service, students, families with visitors. Price-sensitive, often booked the same day.
  • Tourism. Strongly seasonal in most of Canada, with summer peaks that can sell out a fleet, and quiet winters.
  • Trucks and vans. Moves, contractors and small businesses. Higher rates, more wear.

Most successful independents mix two: insurance work fills weekdays and the off-season, retail fills weekends and summer.

2. Register the business

Register your business name provincially, or incorporate federally or provincially, and get a CRA business number. Check your municipality for a business license and zoning on your lot; some municipalities treat a car rental lot differently from an ordinary office. Ask your accountant whether to incorporate before you buy vehicles, because who owns the cars matters for financing, insurance and tax.

3. Get commercial fleet insurance

This is the step that sinks the most start-ups. A personal or ordinary commercial auto policy does not cover renting cars to the public. You need a rental fleet policy from an insurer that writes rental risk, usually through a broker who knows it. Expect underwriters to ask about your rental agreement, how you check drivers' licenses, your minimum age and how you handle damage.

Third-party liability minimums are set by each province. In Atlantic Canada, most sources put the minimum at $200,000 in Newfoundland and Labrador, New Brunswick and Prince Edward Island, and $500,000 in Nova Scotia; many rental fleets carry well above the minimum. Confirm current figures with your broker and provincial regulator. Our guide to insurance for rental fleets in Canada goes further.

4. Register for GST and HST

Once your taxable revenue passes $30,000 over four consecutive calendar quarters, you must register for GST/HST with the CRA, and most rental companies register from day one so they can claim back the tax on the cars they buy. Rentals are taxable at the rate where the car is picked up, which is HST in the Atlantic provinces and Ontario, and GST plus provincial tax elsewhere. Some provinces add their own levies on short-term vehicle rentals; ask your accountant what applies to you.

5. Buy the first cars

Start smaller than you think. Ten well-chosen cars that rent 70% of the time beat thirty that sit. A few rules from operators:

  • Buy what your customers book. For insurance work that's mid-size sedans and small SUVs, matched to what people drive. For tourism it's SUVs and minivans.
  • Buy two to three years old. The steepest depreciation is gone, and the cars still look and drive new.
  • Plan the exit. Know roughly when you'll sell each car. Track what each one earns against what it loses in value each month.
  • Winter tires. In most of Canada, renters expect them from November to April, and Quebec requires them in winter.

6. Set your rates and rules

Price by class and by who's booking: a retail daily rate, weekly and monthly discounts, and agreed rates for insurers and corporate accounts. Raise prices for peak dates, and decide your rules in writing before the first customer: minimum age, deposit, kilometre allowance, fuel policy, where the car may go, late returns and smoking.

7. Agreements, coverages and deposits

Your rental agreement is your protection when something goes wrong. Have a lawyer review it, record the renter's license, photograph the car at pickup and return, and record which coverages the renter accepted or declined every time. A card on file with a hold at pickup protects you against damage and fuel shortfalls. Our guide to replacing paper rental agreements covers doing this without paper.

8. Pick your software

Spreadsheets break at about five cars. Look for software that handles reservations from every source, check-in and return, signed agreements with photos, payments, damage claims, invoices for insurers and body shops, and reports on utilisation and what each car earns. See how fleetr does it.

9. Get your first bookings

  • Visit every body shop and dealership nearby. They need replacement cars for customers every day.
  • Introduce yourself to insurance adjusters with your rates, your fleet and how quickly you can deliver a car.
  • List online. Your Google Business Profile, and a marketplace like fleetr.ai where renters search by city and dates.
  • Answer the phone. Same-day rentals go to whoever picks up.

Start with a niche, insure properly, keep the fleet earning, and let the software carry the paperwork.

Questions people ask

How much does it cost to start a car rental business in Canada?

Most of the cost is the cars and insurance. A small fleet of ten used cars plus a year of fleet insurance, a lot and software is a six-figure commitment, so many operators finance the vehicles and start with a niche such as insurance replacement.

Do I need a special license to rent cars in Canada?

There's no national car rental license. You need to register the business, meet municipal licensing and zoning, register for GST/HST once you pass the threshold, and carry commercial rental fleet insurance.

Is a car rental business profitable?

It can be, when the cars rent often enough. Utilisation, the share of days each car is on rent, is the number that decides it, alongside what each car loses in value.
