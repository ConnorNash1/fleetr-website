Deposits and card holds for car rentals in Canada

A card hold protects you against damage, fuel and late returns without charging the renter up front. Here's how holds work, where they go wrong, and how to set your deposit.

By the fleetr team. November 17, 2026.

What a hold is

A hold, or authorization, asks the renter's card issuer to set aside an amount without charging it. The renter's available credit goes down, but nothing is taken. At return you either charge some or all of it (capture), or let it go (release). Most rental companies hold the rental's estimated total plus a deposit at pickup, then charge the final total at return and release the rest.

How long holds last

Holds don't last forever. Many card authorizations expire after about seven days if they aren't captured, though some card networks and payment processors allow longer holds for car rental businesses (Stripe, for example, offers extended authorizations to eligible businesses). Check your processor's rules. For a rental longer than the hold, plan to charge part of it during the rental, or to place a new hold, rather than finding the authorization gone at return.

Setting your deposit

The deposit is there to cover what the rental's price doesn't: a damage deductible, fuel, a late return, a lost key. Too small and it doesn't protect you; too large and it puts off renters, especially those near their card limit. Many independents set a deposit by class, higher for trucks and luxury cars. Whatever you choose, show it before the renter books, so it's no surprise at the counter.

Debit cards and cash

Holds on debit cards take real money out of the renter's bank account until they're released, which can take days and causes anger when it does. Some debit cards can't be held at all. Many rental companies require a credit card for the hold and accept debit or cash for the final payment only. If you do accept debit for holds, say clearly how long the money may be held.

Charging at return

At return, work out the final total (days, extensions, fuel, kilometres, coverages, taxes), charge it against the hold, and release the rest straight away. Send the renter a receipt the same day. If damage needs an estimate first, charge the rental itself now and the damage separately once you have the estimate, under your terms, rather than holding the whole amount for weeks.

Avoiding chargebacks

  • Have the renter agree to your terms for the deposit and damage charges at pickup, with their signature.
  • Keep pickup and return photos and the agreement with every charge.
  • Charge promptly, and send an itemized receipt.
  • Answer disputes quickly with the agreement, photos and receipt; your processor will ask for them.

What to tell renters

Most complaints about holds come from surprise, not the hold itself. Tell renters three things before pickup, ideally in the booking confirmation:

  1. How much will be held: the estimated rental plus your deposit, as one figure.
  2. When it's released: right after return, and that their bank may take a few days to show it.
  3. What it can be used for: fuel, extra days, damage under the agreement, and nothing else.

For example: "At pickup we'll hold $420 on your card: your rental estimate of $270 and a $150 deposit. At return we charge the final total and release the rest straight away; your bank may take a few days to show it." Renters who know that number at booking rarely argue about it at the counter.

Questions people ask

How much is a typical car rental deposit in Canada?

It varies by company and class. Many independents set a few hundred dollars for cars and more for trucks and luxury vehicles, shown before booking.

How long does a car rental hold stay on my card?

Until the company charges or releases it at return. Unused authorizations also expire on their own, often after about seven days, though rules vary by card and processor.

Can I rent a car with a debit card?

Some companies accept debit for payment but want a credit card for the hold. Each listing on fleetr.ai shows the payment methods the company takes.

Hold at pickup, charge at return.

Save a card at booking, hold the estimate and your deposit at pickup, and charge the final total when the rental closes.
