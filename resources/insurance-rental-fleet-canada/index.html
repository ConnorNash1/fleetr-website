Insurance for rental fleets in Canada

Insurance is the biggest risk and one of the biggest costs in running a rental fleet. Here is how it works for Canadian rental companies, and what underwriters look for.

By the fleetr team. November 17, 2026.

This guide explains the moving parts in plain language. It is not insurance or legal advice: talk to a broker who writes rental fleet risk, and confirm figures with your provincial regulator.

Why you need a rental fleet policy

A rental company's cars are driven by strangers, every day, for short periods. Ordinary commercial auto policies are not written for that, and many exclude renting to the public outright. A rental fleet policy covers the fleet while it's rented and while it sits on your lot, and is priced on your fleet size, where you operate, who you rent to and how you control the risk.

Expect a rental fleet policy to bring together third-party liability, accident benefits as your province requires, and physical damage to your own vehicles (collision and comprehensive), with a deductible you choose. Some insurers also offer garage or premises coverage for your lot.

Third-party liability minimums by province

Every province sets a minimum amount of third-party liability for vehicles on its roads. Most provinces set it at $200,000; Nova Scotia is higher at $500,000, and Quebec's system works differently. In Atlantic Canada that typically means:

  • Newfoundland and Labrador: $200,000 minimum
  • Nova Scotia: $500,000 minimum
  • New Brunswick: $200,000 minimum
  • Prince Edward Island: $200,000 minimum

These are minimums. Most rental fleets carry $1 million or $2 million, because a serious claim can far exceed the minimum, and corporate and insurance customers often require it. Confirm current limits with your broker; regulations change.

The coverages you sell to renters

Most rental companies offer renters optional protection products: a damage waiver that limits what the renter pays if the car is damaged, and sometimes supplementary liability, personal accident or roadside cover. How you can sell these, and what you must disclose, varies by province, so have your wording reviewed.

Whatever you offer, two things protect you: the same offer, worded the same way, on every rental, and a record of what the renter accepted or declined. Disputes about coverage almost always come down to "nobody told me". fleetr shows your coverages in your own wording and records each choice on the agreement.

Renters' own insurance and credit cards

Many renters decline your damage waiver because their personal auto policy or credit card covers rental cars. Sometimes it does, often with limits, exclusions for trucks and luxury cars, or a requirement to pay first and claim later. Ask renters who decline to confirm it, and keep a card on file so a damage charge doesn't depend on chasing them.

For insurance replacement rentals, the claimant's insurer usually pays the daily rate up to a limit, and the renter remains responsible for damage unless coverage says otherwise. Record the claim number, the adjuster and the approved limit on the booking.

When a car comes back damaged

  1. Compare with pickup. Timestamped photos at pickup and return settle most disputes before they start.
  2. Open a claim on the rental with the photos, the agreement and the renter's coverage choice attached.
  3. Get an estimate from a body shop and decide whether to charge the renter, claim on their coverage, or claim on your own policy.
  4. Track it to resolution. Unresolved claims are money left on the table. fleetr keeps each claim open until it's settled.

Keeping premiums down

  • Check every driver. Scan the license, match the name to the booking, enforce your minimum age.
  • Photograph every pickup and return. Underwriters like evidence; it lowers the cost of claims.
  • Hold a deposit or card. Fewer unpaid damage and fuel charges.
  • Keep the fleet maintained. Track service by kilometres and keep cars due for maintenance off rent.
  • Show your record. Clean, organized records of agreements, claims and maintenance help at renewal.

Questions people ask

Can I rent out my car under a personal auto policy?

Generally no. Personal policies exclude renting to the public. Speak to a broker about a rental fleet or peer-to-peer policy before renting any car.

What liability coverage do Canadian rental companies carry?

Each province sets a minimum, $200,000 in most provinces and $500,000 in Nova Scotia, but most rental fleets carry $1 million or more.

Do renters have to buy the rental company's damage waiver?

No, it's optional. Renters often rely on their own auto policy or credit card instead, so record each decision on the agreement.
