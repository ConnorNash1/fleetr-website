When to sell a rental car: depreciation and fleet rotation

Every rental car has a point where keeping it costs more than it earns. Selling just before that point, rather than after, is one of the biggest levers on a rental company's profit.

By the fleetr team. November 17, 2026.

Watch what each car loses

A car's biggest cost is usually the value it loses, not fuel or repairs. Record what you paid for each car and check its market value every few months. The difference, spread over the months you've owned it, is what it's costing you just to keep it. That loss is steep in the first year or two of a car's life and flattens after, which is why many independents buy cars that are already a couple of years old.

Against what it earns

For each car, compare its rental income over the last few months against its running costs (insurance, maintenance, repairs, cleaning) and the value it lost. That gives a net figure per month. A car that's positive and steady is worth keeping. A car whose net is falling, because it rents less or costs more to keep running, is telling you something.

A worked example

A mid-size SUV was bought for $32,000 two years ago and is worth about $23,000 today: it has lost $9,000, or $375 a month. Over the last three months it brought in $1,250 a month in rentals and cost $420 a month in insurance, maintenance and cleaning. Its net is $1,250 minus $420 minus $375, or $455 a month. Worth keeping.

A compact bought at the same time tells a different story. It now rents 14 days a month instead of 22, brings in $690 a month, and needed $600 of brakes and tires last month on top of its usual $350. Its value loss is smaller, around $250 a month, but its net is close to zero and falling. Selling it this spring, and putting the money into another SUV, is the better use of the cash.

Doing this for every car once a quarter takes minutes when the figures come from your rentals rather than a spreadsheet.

The signals to sell

  • Rising repair costs. Brakes, tires and a list of small faults, every few weeks, eat the margin quickly.
  • High kilometres. Resale value drops at certain thresholds, and renters notice a tired car. Many fleets sell somewhere between 60,000 and 120,000 km, depending on the car.
  • Falling demand for the class. If the class sits while others sell out, sell cars in it and buy what's in demand.
  • Damage history. A car with repeated damage claims may be worth selling before the next one.
  • Warranty ending. Many operators sell before the manufacturer's warranty runs out, to avoid uncovered repairs.

Timing the sale

Used car prices move with the season. Selling in spring, ahead of your own quiet period, often gets a better price than selling in late fall, and frees cash before you buy for summer. Don't sell a car in the middle of your busiest weeks unless it's costing you more than a lost rental would.

Planning rotation

Rather than deciding car by car, plan a rotation: sell a few of the oldest cars each quarter and buy a few newer ones. Your fleet's average age stays steady, you avoid selling many cars at once in a weak market, and buying is spread across the year. Take each car off rent the day you decide to sell, so it isn't booked out while you're showing it to buyers.

Questions people ask

How many kilometres before a rental company sells a car?

It varies with the car and market, but many fleets sell somewhere between 60,000 and 120,000 km, and earlier if repair costs climb.

Should rental companies buy new or used cars?

Many independents buy cars two to three years old, after the steepest drop in value, so less of their money is lost to depreciation.

How do I know if a rental car is losing money?

Compare its rental income with its running costs and the value it loses each month. If the net is falling or negative, it's time to sell.

Know what every car is worth to you.

Purchase price, market value, depreciation and net income per car, from the rentals you already run.
