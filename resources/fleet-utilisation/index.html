Rental fleet utilisation: how to measure it and raise it

Utilisation is the share of the time your cars are earning. It's the number that most decides whether a rental company makes money, and most independents don't track it.

By the fleetr team. November 17, 2026.

How to measure it

Time utilisation is simple: days on rent divided by days available, for a period. A car that was on rent 21 of 30 days ran at 70%. Measure it per class and per branch, not just for the whole fleet, because a fleet average hides the class that's always sold out and the one that sits.

Decide what counts as available. Most operators leave out days a car spent in maintenance or a body shop, and track those separately, because they're a different problem with a different fix.

A worked example

Take a branch with 20 cars over a 30-day month: 600 car-days. Two cars spent five days each in the body shop, so 590 days were available. The cars were on rent for 413 days in total. Utilisation is 413 divided by 590, or 70%.

Split by class, the picture changes. The six small SUVs ran at 88% and turned away bookings on three weekends. The eight compact cars ran at 55%. The fleet average of 70% hid both: the SUVs needed a higher rate or another car, and two of the compacts could be sold or moved to a busier branch. Raising the SUVs to full and the compacts to 65% would add about 40 rental days a month without buying a single car.

Do the same split by month and you'll see your seasons, which tells you when to add cars and when to sell them.

What a good rate looks like

There's no single right number; it depends on your market and season. Tourism fleets swing from near 100% in summer to far less in winter, while insurance replacement fleets run steadier. What matters is your trend, and how each class compares with the others. A class well above the rest is underpriced or too small. A class well below is overpriced, too big or in the wrong place.

Faster turnaround

Every hour a car spends between renters is an hour it can't earn. Common culprits: returns that aren't logged until someone walks the lot, cleaning done in the order cars came back rather than the order they're needed, and cars waiting for a check-in agent. Letting renters log their own return, and cleaning first the cars needed for the next pickups, both shorten the gap.

The right mix of cars

Look at utilisation by class over a few months. If minivans are full every weekend while compact cars sit, shift the next purchases towards minivans and sell the oldest compacts. Small changes in mix move utilisation more than any amount of discounting.

Move cars to the demand

With more than one branch, the same class can be sold out at one location and idle at the other. Transferring cars between branches ahead of a busy week is cheaper than turning renters away. Plan transfers from your availability a week or two out, not on the day.

Count the bookings you lose

Utilisation only tells you about the cars you had. Lost demand tells you about the cars you didn't: the searches for dates when a class was sold out, the calls you turned away. If a class is losing bookings every weekend, you know exactly what to buy next. Track it alongside utilisation.

Questions people ask

How do you calculate fleet utilisation?

Divide the days your cars were on rent by the days they were available in the period. Most operators leave out days in maintenance and track those separately.

What is a good utilisation rate for a car rental company?

It depends on your market and season. Watch your trend and compare classes; the class far above or below the others tells you what to change.

Does lowering prices raise utilisation?

Sometimes, but changing your fleet mix, turnaround and transfers often raises it more without lowering your revenue per day.
