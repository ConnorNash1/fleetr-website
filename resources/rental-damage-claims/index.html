Damage claims at a rental company, from return to payment

Damage is where rental companies most often lose money they're owed. A clear process, from the photos at pickup to the final payment, recovers far more of it.

By the fleetr team. November 17, 2026.

It starts at pickup

You can't prove new damage without proof of the old. Photograph every car at every pickup, all four sides and anything already damaged, with the time recorded. Damage already on file should carry forward to the next rental, so the next renter isn't blamed for it and the record stays honest. Renter self check-in on a phone does this without tying up your staff.

At return

Photograph the car again at return, in the same way. Compare with pickup before the renter leaves if you can; a conversation at the counter settles far more than an email a week later. If the renter returns the car after hours, their own return photos, with the time locked in, are your evidence.

Opening the claim

Open a claim on the rental straight away, while it's fresh: what the damage is, where on the car, the pickup and return photos, and the renter's coverage choice from the agreement. Note whether the car can still be rented or has to come off the road. A claim that lives in someone's notebook is a claim that gets forgotten.

Getting the estimate

Get a repair estimate from a body shop. For small damage, some operators use a published price list for common repairs (a chip, a scuff, a small dent), which is faster and easier to explain to the renter. Keep the estimate with the claim.

Recovering the cost

Who pays depends on what the renter chose and what they're covered by:

  • The renter accepted your damage waiver: they pay up to the waiver's terms (often nothing, or a deductible), and you absorb or claim the rest as your waiver allows.
  • The renter declined it: they're responsible under your agreement. Charge the card on file for the amount your terms allow, or send an invoice, with the photos and estimate attached.
  • Their own insurance or credit card covers rentals: give them what they need to claim (the agreement, photos, estimate and invoice), but don't wait on it to get paid if your terms let you charge them.
  • Your own fleet policy: for large damage, a claim on your policy may make sense, weighed against the deductible and your premiums.

Charging a card without the renter's agreement to your terms invites a chargeback, so make sure your rental terms, signed at pickup, cover damage charges and the deposit. Our guide to deposits and card holds covers this.

Talking to the renter

How you tell a renter about damage decides whether they pay or dispute it. Show them, don't tell them: the pickup photo of that corner of the car, then the return photo. Explain what happens next (an estimate, then a charge or invoice under the terms they signed) and when. Stay calm and factual; most renters accept clear evidence, and the ones who don't will dispute however you put it.

Put it in writing the same day: what was found, the photos, what the agreement says about damage, and what you'll charge once you have an estimate. A written record made at the time is worth far more than a phone call remembered weeks later, if the charge is ever disputed with the card issuer.

Tracking it to the end

Keep every claim open until it's paid or written off, and look at open claims every week. Once a month, check the total recovered against the total repaired. If you're recovering much less than you spend, the gap is usually missing pickup photos, unsigned coverage choices or claims that were never followed up.

Questions people ask

Can a rental company charge my card for damage?

If the rental agreement you signed allows it and the damage is documented, usually yes, up to what the agreement and your coverage choice allow.

What proof do I need for a rental damage claim?

Timestamped photos at pickup and return, the signed agreement with the renter's coverage choice, and a repair estimate.

What if the renter's credit card covers rental damage?

Give them the agreement, photos, estimate and invoice to claim with, but collect under your own terms rather than waiting on their claim.

Claims that never go missing.

Photos at pickup and return, each claim on its rental, open until it's settled.
