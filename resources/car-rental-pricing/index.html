How to price car rentals: daily, weekly and monthly rates

Rental pricing is a balance between filling cars and not giving away your best days. Here's a practical way to set and adjust rates as an independent rental company in Canada.

By the fleetr team. November 17, 2026.

  2. Price by class, not by car
  3. Price by who's booking
  4. Weekly and monthly rentals
  5. Peaks and quiet weeks

Most independent rental companies set prices by looking at the competition and coming in a little lower. That's a reasonable start, but it leaves money on the table on busy days and doesn't protect you on slow ones. A better approach starts with your own costs.

Start from what a car costs you

Add up what each car costs you in a month: financing or the value it loses, insurance, maintenance, cleaning, registration and a share of your rent and staff. Divide by the days you expect it to be on rent. If a car costs $900 a month and rents 20 days, every rental day has to bring in at least $45 before you make anything. That number is your floor, not your price.

The days it rents matter as much as the rate. The same car at 25 days a month only needs $36 a day to cover itself. This is why utilisation and pricing have to be looked at together.

Price by class, not by car

Renters book a class (small car, mid-size SUV, minivan), not a particular car. Set one daily rate per class, with clear steps between them, so moving a renter up a class is an easy upsell. Keep the number of classes small enough that every class has several cars in it; a class with one car sells out the moment it's booked.

Price by who's booking

Different customers pay differently, and your rates should reflect it:

  • Walk-in and online renters pay your retail rate. Keep online and walk-in prices the same, so nobody books online and then argues at the counter.
  • Insurers usually approve a daily limit for a claim. Know the common limits and keep a class that fits each one.
  • Body shops and dealerships often pay a negotiated rate in return for steady volume.
  • Corporate accounts trade a lower rate for a commitment, often with invoicing on terms.

Weekly and monthly rentals

Longer rentals cost you less per day: one cleaning, one check-in, no gaps between renters. Pass some of that on. A common pattern is a weekly rate worth five or six daily rates, and a monthly rate well below four weekly rates. Make sure the system applies the cheaper rate when it's better for the renter; nothing loses goodwill faster than a renter finding they'd have paid less by booking a longer rental.

Peaks and quiet weeks

Summer weekends, long weekends, holidays and big local events sell out. Raise rates for those dates in advance, rather than discovering on the Thursday that you're full. In quiet weeks, hold your rate and fill cars with insurance and body shop work instead of discounting retail; discounts train regular customers to wait for them. Watch your availability a few weeks out: when a class is close to selling out, that's the moment to raise its price, not lower it.

Coverages, fuel and kilometres

Coverages are a large share of many independents' profit. Offer them the same way, with the same wording, on every rental, and price them per day. For fuel, decide between "return it full or pay our price per litre" and selling a prepaid tank. For kilometres, unlimited is simplest for local rentals; a daily allowance with a per-kilometre charge protects you on long trips.

Review every month

Once a month, look at revenue per class, utilisation per class and the bookings you turned away. If a class is always full, it's underpriced or you need more of it. If a class sits at 50%, lower the rate, sell some cars or move them to a busier branch.

Questions people ask

How much should I charge per day for a rental car?

Start from what the car costs you each month divided by the days it rents, then set a rate above that by class, and compare with what nearby companies charge for the same class.

Should weekly rentals be cheaper per day?

Usually, yes. Longer rentals cost less to handle, and a weekly rate worth five or six daily rates is common.

Should I discount in slow periods?

Hold your retail rate where you can and fill cars with insurance, body shop and corporate work instead; frequent discounts teach customers to wait for them.

Rates that fill themselves in.

Set your rates by class and source once, with seasons and discounts, and every booking is priced the same way.
