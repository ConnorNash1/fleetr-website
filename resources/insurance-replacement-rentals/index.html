Insurance replacement rentals: working with body shops and adjusters

Insurance replacement work is the steadiest business an independent rental company can have. Here's how it works, how to win it, and how to keep it running without paperwork piling up.

By the fleetr team. November 17, 2026.

When a driver's car is damaged in a covered accident, their policy often pays for a replacement vehicle while it's repaired. Insurers, adjusters and body shops send those renters to rental companies they trust. For an independent, that can mean steady weekday business all year.

How replacement rentals work

The insurer approves a rental for a claim, usually with a daily limit (the most it pays per day) and sometimes a class. The renter picks up a car, and the rental runs until the repair is done. The insurer pays the approved daily amount, normally on account; the renter pays anything above it, plus anything not covered, such as fuel or coverages they choose to add.

Some rentals are booked by the adjuster, some by the body shop, and some by the renter with a claim number in hand. Your process needs to handle all three.

Winning the work

  • Visit every body shop nearby. Shops want their customers in a car quickly, so the car doesn't sit waiting for parts while the customer calls them every day.
  • Be quick. Same-day cars, delivered or ready when the customer drops off their own car, win more referrals than a lower rate.
  • Know the limits. Keep a class that fits the common daily limits, so nobody is surprised at the counter.
  • Make booking easy. A portal where a shop or adjuster books a car with the claim or repair order number saves everyone a phone call.
  • Send clean invoices. Insurers and shops come back to companies whose invoices match the claim the first time.

Taking the booking right

Get these details on every replacement booking before the car goes out: the claim number (or repair order number), the adjuster or shop contact, the approved daily limit or class, the estimated repair days and who pays what. A missing claim number is the most common reason an invoice comes back unpaid.

During the rental

Treat the renter like any other: check their license, photograph the car, record their coverage choices and get a signature. They're often stressed after an accident, so a quick, phone-based check-in helps. Make clear at pickup what the insurer pays and what they pay.

When the repair runs long

Repairs run over all the time: parts are late, the damage is worse than the estimate. Extensions need the insurer's approval, and an extension without approval can become the renter's bill. Make it easy for the shop or adjuster to ask for an extension and for you to approve it, with the new return date on the rental.

Billing and getting paid

Invoice the insurer or shop when the rental closes, with the claim number, renter, dates, days and daily rate on the invoice. Charge the renter's share separately, at return. Track what each insurer and shop owes by age (0 to 30 days, 31 to 60 and so on) and follow up on anything past your terms. Replacement work is only profitable if it's paid.

Common mistakes

  • Putting the renter in a class above the limit without telling them they'll pay the difference.
  • Extending without approval, then finding the insurer won't pay the extra days.
  • Invoicing late, or with the claim number missing, so the invoice sits unpaid.
  • Treating shops as an afterthought. The shop decides where its customers rent; a quick thank-you and a reliable car keep the referrals coming.

Questions people ask

Who pays for an insurance replacement rental?

The insurer pays the approved daily amount for the covered days, usually on account. The renter pays anything above the limit and extras such as fuel or added coverages.

What is a daily limit?

The most an insurer will pay per day for the rental on a claim. Booking a class that fits the limit avoids a bill for the renter.

How do I get body shops to send me renters?

Visit them, have cars ready quickly, make booking and extensions easy, and send invoices that match the claim the first time.

Make it easy for shops and adjusters.

Partners book in their own portal with the claim or repair order number, and you invoice them on terms.
